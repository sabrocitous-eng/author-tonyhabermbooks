01 — The Story
“De eso no se habla — we do not talk about that.”

A sentence heard in many Cuban homes. In Tony Haber-Matos’s work, it becomes a question: what does silence cost?

Born in 1973 in Santiago de Cuba, Tony Haber-Matos grew up in a family that learned to whisper. In that house, certain names, histories, and truths were spoken softly — if they were spoken at all.

Faith offered steadiness. Fear taught caution. And silence, over time, revealed its price: memory thins, stories narrow, and the next generation inherits questions without a place to ask them.

Service in the United States Navy and study at Florida International University sharpened a different discipline — attention, endurance, and the responsibility to carry a story without bending it to comfort.

His work returns to what sustained his family: resilience, faith, heritage, and the belief that a story told honestly can become a kind of inheritance. Not a monument to the past, but a language for the present.

Our Story

The story behind the book

This is a story about help, and what help costs when it is no longer free.

Havana, today. At the Colegio San Jerónimo, three students — Pedro Martínez Pérez, Rafael and Juan — face the university entrance examination that will decide their futures, and fear makes them customers. They pay Eulogio, an unlicensed spiritual practitioner, five hundred dollars each for the examination answers, and Pedro pays his share with his mother’s stolen jewelry, letting the family’s housekeeper, Yadira, take the blame.

Behind Eulogio stands something older and colder: the Northstar Continuity Review, a foundation network built over twelve years to classify children as reliable, uncertain or dangerous, and to convert every act of help into debt, referrals, silence, signatures and other people’s names. Journalist Lucía Valdés begins to pull the thread. The boys’ crime is real, and the novel never excuses it. Its question is what a society becomes when help itself is the instrument of control, and who gets to decide who deserves it.

“In Room 7, offered two answers by a machine, Pedro types a third: I accept responsibility for what I did, I reject responsibility for what you did with it.”
— from LOST INNOCENCES: The Pact

The novel is told in two parts: Story One, “The Examination” (Chapters 1–9), and Story Two, “The Network” (Chapters 10–39). Its themes are faith, fear, and the price of silence; help versus ownership; consent; and the truth that gratitude is not a debt.

About the Author

Tony Haber-Matos

Tony Haber-Matos is a Miami-based author and entrepreneur, founder of Casa Santiago LLC and Qvivo Brands Inc., and a veteran-owned business leader.

His fiction is rooted in faith, family, and memory, shaped by the Cuban exile experience that divided his own family — a family that learned to whisper, and a grandmother’s prayers, a father’s silences, and a mother’s songs from an island left behind.

The first thing the world ever read of his writing was on wine labels: in Richard Blanco’s poetry class at FIU, his wine brands Loco Por Ti and Crazy Love were born, with his poems printed in Spanish and English on the labels. He later revived Guayabita del Pinar, a Cuban spirit brand with a long memory.

He is now writing Part II of the saga, and preparing the February 2027 launch of Sabrocito, his agave spirit, at Total Wine.

05 — Philosophy

Five principles for making work that lasts

01

Write the story only you can tell

If someone else could write it the same way, it is not yet yours.

02

Start with a question, not an outline

A good question keeps a story honest long after the plan runs out.

03

Let characters fail

Failure is where character is proved — and where a reader begins to trust.

04

Don’t wait for permission

Build the house. Publish the book. Pour the first glass.

05

End honestly

A neat ending is easy. A true ending is the one that stays.

A movement, not a transaction

A fund for scholarships that ask for nothing back.

Help should not arrive with conditions. The work ahead is to create support that trusts young people first — and lets them decide what their story becomes.

Give without a leash. Education, dignity, possibility.
